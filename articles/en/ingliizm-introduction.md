## General Information

In Da'Aryan writing, this image was represented by a single symbol, a [t'raga](https://derzhavarus.ru/tragi-daarijskie.html); in Kh'Aryan writing, it was represented by the fundamental [rune](https://derzhavarus.ru/runy-karuna.html) Ynglia.

The meaning our Wise Ancestors gave to the original image of Ynglia was always the Divine Primordial Fire of Creation, within which diverse forms of Life appeared in the Endless Infinities brought forth in the New Reality, that is, in numerous universes.

The original Sacred Light of Ynglia, emanating from the Primordial Creator, whom we [Old Believers](https://derzhavarus.ru/starovery.html) call [Great Ra-M-Kha](https://derzhavarus.ru/ra-m-ha.html), also filled our blessed Midgard-Earth. For us, the Old Believer Ynglings who profess the Ancient Faith of our First Ancestors, Ra-M-Kha is the One Supreme Creator. ## Outward Signs of the Faith

According to our most ancient traditions, sacred writings, and inherited accounts, during invocations and hymns, and when entering our temples and sanctuaries, we make the [Holy Sign](https://derzhavarus.ru/perunica-molniya.html), also called the Sign of the God Perun or Perunitsa (lightning). To make it, the tips of three fingers of the right hand — the thumb, ring finger, and little finger — are joined in honor of the Great Triglav of the World of Yav: Svarog, Perun, and Sventovit, who embody Conscience, Freedom (Will), and Light. The index and middle fingers are held straight together, representing the Heavenly Kin and Lada the Mother of the Gods.

We then place these two fingers first on the forehead, then on the eyes — the left eye followed by the right — and finally on the lips. Through this Holy Sign we illuminate our Holy Old Faith, Yngliism, repeating the holy lightning of the God Perun, which sanctified the blessed lives of all our Ancestors and sanctifies our everyday lives today.

We place the Holy Sign on the forehead to sanctify the mind that comprehends the Wisdom of our Gods and Ancestors; on the eyes to sanctify our sight and see their true Creation; and on the lips to sanctify our speech, especially when speaking the Divine Word and Divine Wisdom, and to keep our lips from blasphemy.

Bowing the head during invocations and hymns expresses respect for our Ancient Ancestral Gods and our Holy-Wise and Great Ancestors, in gratitude for their help in all our deeds and creative work.

“Glory to our Gods and Ancestors!”

## The Symbol of the Old Faith

The principal symbol of the Old Faith, both in ancient times and today, is the [Star of Ynglia](https://derzhavarus.ru/zvezda-inglii-simvol-staroj-very.html) (Fig. 5). It symbolizes not only the Original Pure Light — the Primordial Fire of Divine Creation and the Radiant Light of our Yarilo-Sun — but also the harmonious White Human Being, a descendant of the Ancient Bright Gods.

The three triangles representing the Divine Principle signify one of the [Great Triglavs](https://derzhavarus.ru/velikie-triglavy-mirov.html), which watches over a Divine World: Yav, Nav, Slav, or Prav. The outer circle enclosing the Great Triglav represents the One Life-Giving Ynglia. The infinite space outside the circle speaks of the One Creator, whose name is Great Ra-M-Kha.

The three triangles representing the Human Principle signify a Healthy Body, a Strong Spirit, and a Bright Soul; the outer circle enclosing them signifies a Pure Conscience. The infinite space outside the circle symbolizes the many-sided Divine World in which the descendant of the Ancient Bright Gods — the Human Being — lives and creates.

The three triangles representing the Natural Principle signify Earth, Water, and Fire; the outer circle enclosing them signifies Air. The infinite space outside the circle symbolizes the Most Pure Svarga, that is, the Heavens.

Additional accompanying symbols are often placed at the center of the Star of Ynglia. For example, a sword pointing downward (Fig. 4) signifies the preservation of Ancient Wisdom. A sword pointing upward signifies the defense of all clans and lands of the Holy Race against external and internal enemies. Community members who wear this symbol belong to Slavic and Aryan communities that preserve Ancient Wisdom and pass it from generation to generation.

A Perunitsa placed at the center of Ynglia (Fig. 6) symbolizes the protection and patronage of the God Perun. Amulets with a Perunitsa are generally worn by members of communities that honor and glorify the Most Wise [God Perun](http://derzhavarus.ru/bog-perun.html), Patron of the Slavic Clans.

The Star of Ynglia combined with the central symbol our Ancestors originally called Vestnik, “the Messenger,” brings Health, Happiness, and Joy. This ancient amulet of happiness (Fig. 7) is sometimes colloquially called Mati-Gotka, meaning “Mother of the Goths.”

Community members who wear the protective symbolism of Mati-Gotka belong to Slavic and Aryan communities in which various forms of Ancient Culture and Tradition are preserved and passed from generation to generation.

Some Priests, Elders, and Heads of Slavic and Aryan communities, besides body-worn clan or community amulets and pectoral protective symbols worn over clothing, also wear pectoral sources of Spiritual and Natural Power called artakonas.

An artakona combines two powerful forces of the living world: the claw of a rodovich [3], an animal representing the clan's natural patron symbol, and beads made from sacred wood. ## Bereginya

Since ancient times, our Ancestors bound their hair around the forehead with embroidered braid, ribbon, or a leather cord, which they called a Bereginya (Fig. 9).

Our Wise Ancestors believed that the Bereginya performed several tasks at once: it protected the mind from confusion and bad thoughts; guarded the Soul and body against curses and the evil eye; kept the hair from being blown about or falling into the eyes; and prevented sweat from obscuring the eyes during work.

## Fasting

The periods for cleansing the human body — [fasts](https://derzhavarus.ru/post-ochishenie.html) — are calculated only according to the [Da'Aryan Krugolet of Chislobog](http://derzhavarus.ru/krugolet-chisloboga-tablicy-primery.html).

The word POST (ПОСТЪ) itself is an abbreviation of an ancient common Slavic symbolic expression, formed from the initial letters of its words:

- П — polnoye: complete;
- О — ochishcheniye: cleansing;
- С — sobstvennykh: of one's own;
- Т — teles: bodies;
- Ъ — sotvoryasha: bringing about.

This system of fasting in the Old Russian Yngliist Church of Orthodox Old Believer Ynglings allows our body to cleanse itself and adjust from one kind of food to another.

Fasts observed by all community members and parishioners of the Old Russian Yngliist Church of Orthodox Old Believer Ynglings:

### The Holy Fast

Nine days, one Slavic week, from Ramkhat 22 to Ramkhat 31.

During this period, Orthodox Old Believer Ynglings are not permitted to eat meat, fish, fatty protein foods (eggs, butter, milk), or spicy food.

On these days, Old Believers should eat vegetables, fruit, berries, grains, and legumes in the morning and evening, and drink water, juices, and herbal teas during the daytime.

This prepares the body for the transition to foods preserved early in the season.

### Dazhdbog's Fast

Eighteen days, two full Slavic weeks, from Beyleg 1 to 18.

During this period, Orthodox Old Believer Ynglings prepare for the transition from foods preserved early in the season to fully preserved foods, such as salted and smoked products.

During the fast, Old Believers are not permitted to eat meat, fatty fish, eggs, white bread, rich dairy products (cream and sour cream), or butter. Vegetable oil is permitted.

Among bread products, “gray” and rye bread may be eaten, especially bread made from a midnight sponge starter. Other foods are consumed as in the Holy Fast.

Sauerkraut or buckwheat porridge is recommended during the daytime to improve metabolism.

### The Pure Fast

Eighteen days, two full Slavic weeks, from Geylet 22 to 40.

During this period, Old Believers are not permitted to eat meat, fatty foods, sour or fermented foods, or eggs.

All animal fats and vegetable oils are forbidden during the Pure Fast. Permitted foods include lean fish (except fried fish), steamed grain porridges, pasta, “gray” and rye bread, nuts, berries, juices, herbal teas, and small quantities of legumes.

In the second week of the Pure Fast, fresh plant foods, such as green onions and radishes, are introduced gradually.

### The Great Fast

Thirteen days, one and a half Slavic weeks, from Daylet 22 to 35.

During this period, fatty meat foods (except chicken and rabbit), fatty fish, butter and other fats (except vegetable oil), and rich dairy products (cream and sour cream) are not permitted. All other low-fat foods may be eaten.

### The Bright Fast

Eighteen days, two full Slavic weeks, from Veylet 33 to Kheylet 9.

During this period, only plant foods may be eaten, and only in the morning and evening.

On the [Holy Day of the God Kupala](https://derzhavarus.ru/den-boga-kupala.html), which coincides with the Bright Fast, meat of any fat content may be eaten, but it must be cooked over Living Fire: a campfire lit from ritually produced Living Fire.

### The One-Day Fast

Every seventh day of the nine-day week, in accordance with the [Commandment of Svarog](https://derzhavarus.ru/zapovedi-bogov.html): “Honor three days of the week — the third, seventh, and ninth…” On this day, it is recommended to abstain from food entirely, except for juices, water, fruit compote without the berries, herbal teas, and kissel. If the one-day fast falls on a holiday, we celebrate the holiday rather than fast.

Fasts are not observed by children under twelve, pregnant and nursing mothers, men and women over sixty, warriors on guard duty, or the wounded and ill, since they need nourishment to gain additional strength and health.

These Nine Foundations of Yngliism were always simple and natural in essence, yet essential for the development of Spirit, Soul, and creativity among the people of the clans of the Great Race.

The people of the Great Race are direct descendants of the Ancient Bright Gods who inhabit the Divine World of Prav and all the Heavenly Halls of the Most Pure Svarga.

So that the descendants of the clans of the Great Race, and their descendants in turn, would not stray from the Golden Path toward perfection of Spirit and Soul, the Priests serving the Heavenly Gods compiled these Nine Foundations of Faith:

1. ENLIGHTENMENT — The first foundation tells us to study the Sacred Writings and Sacred Traditions, the Wisdom of our Ancestors, and the Wisdom of other human clans; to fill our lives with holiness and good deeds in glory of our Gods and Ancestors.

2. SPIRITUALITY — The second foundation tells us that every person should develop the spiritual side of life and introduce others to Spirituality.

3. COMPASSION — The third foundation tells us that every person should have compassion for all living things created by the Gods.

4. INNER PEACE — The fourth foundation tells us that every person should strive for harmony among their [bodies](https://derzhavarus.ru/9-tel-cheloveka.html), [Soul](https://derzhavarus.ru/dusha-krug-absoluta.html), and Spirit, for only then can they find inner peace.

5. PATIENCE — The fifth foundation tells us to be understanding of others' actions, since every person is FREE, but personal freedom ends where another person's freedom begins. No personal Freedom can stand above duty to one's clan and the Heavenly Gods.

6. PEACEFULNESS — The sixth foundation tells us to show peacefulness toward all human clans, while not sparing the lives of enemies who enter our lands with evil intent and weapons.

7. LOVE OF ONE'S NEIGHBOR — The seventh foundation tells us to treat all living things created by the Gods with love and kindness, and likewise to honor the memory of our Ancestors and the History of human clans.

8. TRIAL — The eighth foundation tells us that, to reach Heavenly Vyriy and Heavenly Asgard, we must follow the path of spiritual perfection. The trials in our lives are given to test our Spiritual Strength.

9. PURPOSEFULNESS — The ninth foundation tells us that everything in life, and Life itself, has meaning, a calling, and a purpose. Every person should strive toward their goal to fulfill their calling and give their life a Higher Spiritual Meaning.
